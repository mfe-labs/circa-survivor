1Win percentage

A sportsbook's two prices on a game always imply more than 100% between them. The excess is the book's margin, and it has to come out.

implied = 100/(price+100) if the price is positive = −price/(−price+100) if negative for each book: fair = implied ÷ (implied home + implied away) W% (home) = median of the books' fair numbers W% (away) = 1 − W% (home)

The median rather than the average, so one book that is slow to move its price cannot drag the number. The away side is taken as the complement rather than its own median, because a median of the two sides separately need not add to 100%.

A game needs both prices from the same book to count. Spreads and power ratings never produce a win percentage. Fewer than three books quoting is marked with an amber dot; one book is marked provisional; none leaves the cell blank.

Lions at Bills, Week 2

DraftKings: Bills −250, Lions +205. Implied 250/350 = 71.4% and 100/305 = 32.8%, which sum to 104.2%. Dividing both by 1.042 gives Bills 68.5%.

The four books that quoted gave 68.2%, 68.5%, 68.9% and 69.0%. The median of those is 68.7%, so the Lions are 31.3%.

2Power ratings

One number per team, in points, relative to an average team on a neutral field. They exist to project games the books have not priced yet.

every 2026 game with a betting line gives one equation: rating(home) − rating(away) + 2 = spread solve all of them at once by least squares, with ridge shrinkage λ=2 pulling each rating toward a prior

The prior is the Super Bowl futures market. Each book's title odds are de-vigged into probabilities, averaged across books, converted to log-odds, then standardised and scaled to ±3.5 points. It is the market's own view of who is good this season, so early-season ratings do not lean on last year's results. It refreshes every three days.

Shrinkage matters early and fades: with 48 games of lines the fit is still partly the prior, and by midseason the games dominate. A projected margin becomes a win chance through a normal curve with a standard deviation of 13.5 points, the usual figure for NFL scoring.

Current top: Rams 5.6, Bills 4.7, Ravens 4.1. Bottom: Titans −5.3, Browns −6.4, Dolphins −7.4.

3Future value

How many strong-favorite weeks a team still has after the week you are viewing.

for each later week: weight = 1 / (1 + e^−(win − 0.65)/0.05) future value = sum of those weights

The curve is centred at 65% with a width of 5 points, so a week counts nearly fully at 80%, about 0.88 at 75%, half at 65%, 0.12 at 55% and nothing at 45%. Weeks the books have priced use the real win percentage; the rest use the power-rating projection.

The number reads as a count of usable weeks, which is what makes teams comparable. The earlier version summed margins above 60% and could not tell a team with two usable weeks from one with none.

4Pick popularity

Once a week locks, Circa publishes a PDF of all 25,000 entries and their picks, and the app counts them. Before it locks, this model estimates them.

score = win^a × e^(−b × future value) × e^(−c × holiday pressure) × availability P% = score ÷ (sum of scores over teams favored that week)
  • win^a amplifies small differences in win chance, because the field concentrates on the safest-looking team far harder than the odds alone imply. At a = 11, ten extra points of win chance make a team roughly three times more attractive.
  • e^(−b × future value) marks down teams the field is saving. At b = 0.26 each point of future value costs about 23%.
  • e^(−c × holiday pressure) marks down teams the field should be holding for a holiday week. Pressure is how scarce that week's pool is (1 ÷ the teams still broadly available to the field) times how close the week is, decayed 0.8 per week, and it drops to zero once the week passes. For a team in both pools that is a 4% cut in Week 3 and a 21% cut by Week 11, at c = 2.
  • availability is the share of surviving entries that have not already used the team, derived from Circa's past results. Near 1.00 in September, a major factor by midseason.
  • Teams below 50% win chance score zero.

All three constants are fitted, not chosen: a search over every week that has Circa's actual numbers, scoring each candidate by the total error across teams, weighted by each team's real share so the big picks matter most. A penalty holds them near 8, 0.15 and 0, measured against a plausible spread for each, and weakens on its own as weeks accumulate. They currently sit at a = 11, b = 0.26, c = 0 on two weeks of data. The holiday weight is at zero because September data cannot see the field saving November teams; if that behaviour shows up as the holidays approach, the fit will pick it up.

5EV

Win chance divided by how much of the field survives alongside you.

S(i) = P(i) + Σ over all other games of P(j) × W(j) raw = W(i) ÷ S(i) EV = raw ÷ (pick-weighted mean of raw), so the field averages 1.00

S is the expected share of the field still alive next week given your team wins. Your own backers count in full, since they survive exactly when you do. Your opponent's backers count as nothing. Every other game contributes its popularity times its win chance.

S barely varies between teams, sitting between 0.78 and 0.80 across Week 2, because it is dominated by the other fifteen games. That makes EV close to win chance divided by 0.78. Popularity is a smaller correction than people expect: ten points of it costs about 2% of EV, while ten points of win chance is worth about 12%. The reason is that a crowd on a heavy favorite was surviving anyway. As the field shrinks and concentrates, S starts to vary more and popularity matters more.

If some games in the week have no prices, EV is computed from the rest and the header reads EV*. Below 75% of games priced it is blank, because the missing teams flatter everyone else.

6DILI

EV, charged for what the pick costs you later. Calculated separately for each entry, since it depends on which teams that entry has already used.

DILI = EV ÷ B^k × H

B, the future forfeit. For every week after this one:

bar = mean win chance of the entry's 3 best other available teams edge = this team's win chance − bar (skip if ≤ 0) s = 0.8^(weeks away) B = exp( Σ s × ln(1 + edge ÷ bar) )

The bar is the top three rather than the best one, because you rarely take the single biggest favorite and nobody can identify it months out. The 0.8 discount is the chance of still being alive to use the week. B comes out slightly above 1, and reads as the fraction by which the team beats your realistic alternatives.

k, the exponent. Style multiplied by a calendar schedule.

Early weeks weight the future heavily; the last three barely at all, since there is nothing left to save for. In Week 2 on the Future style, k = 2.03, which turns a forfeit of 1.07 into about 13% off.

H, holiday scarcity. Thanksgiving has 10 eligible teams and Christmas 8, with six in both. An entry that runs out of eligible teams has no legal pick that week, which is a loss.

for each holiday week still ahead that the team plays in: n = eligible teams the entry still has factor = ((n−1) ÷ n)^(0.5 × style) and 0 when n = 1 H = the factors multiplied together

Eligibility only. A holiday underdog is still a body in the pool, so a team is charged the same whether it is favored that day or not. On the Future style the charge is 8% per full pool, 24% at a pool of three, 37% at two, and total on the last one.

B's shape, k's schedule, the top-three bar and the holiday exponent are all chosen rather than fitted. There is no ground truth to fit them against, only outcomes over a season.
